POS and shop software questions, answered

For shops, pharmacies, bars and restaurants comparing till systems in Uganda, including how they behave when power or internet goes off.

What is POS software?

Point of sale software is the program that records what a shop sells, at what price, to which customer, and what stock is left. It replaces the paper receipt book and the calculator, and gives the owner a daily sales figure without counting cash by hand.

How much does POS software cost in Uganda?

A single-till system typically costs UGX 900,000 to 2,500,000 one-off, or UGX 60,000 to 150,000 a month as a subscription. A pharmacy with expiry tracking, credit customers and multiple branches runs higher. A thermal printer costs UGX 250,000 to 450,000.

What is the best POS software in Uganda?

The one that keeps selling when the internet drops, prints a receipt in seconds, and shows the owner the day's sales on their phone. Test it with a real queue at 6pm, not in a quiet office, and check who supports it on a Sunday.

Does a POS work without internet?

A proper one does. Sales are saved on the machine and uploaded when the connection returns, so a whole day of selling continues offline. Cloud-only tills that stop at the first network drop are the reason many Ugandan shops still fear POS systems.

Can it print receipts on a thermal printer?

Yes, and it should print the school or shop name, the item lines, the total, any discount and a receipt number that can be reprinted later. Receipt reprinting matters when a customer returns goods or accounts do not balance at closing.

Does POS software manage stock and expiry dates?

Good software does. It deducts stock as items are sold, warns when a product drops below a set level, and flags batches close to expiry, which is essential for pharmacies and clinics. Stock takes then compare what the system expects with what is on the shelf.

Can the till accept MTN and Airtel money?

Yes. Mobile money collection can be requested from the till, and the sale is marked paid once the payment confirms, so the cashier cannot fake a confirmation. Card payments are possible through a bank or gateway integration if your volumes justify it.

Do I have to buy new hardware?

Usually not. Most systems run on a laptop or desktop you already own, plus a thermal printer and a barcode scanner if you sell packaged goods. A complete starter setup in Uganda costs about UGX 700,000 to 1,200,000 in hardware.

How is a POS different from a normal till or calculator?

A till adds money; a POS remembers what sold. That difference gives you stock levels, profit per item, best sellers, staff performance and a daily summary you can read from your phone, none of which a cash drawer can tell you.

Can I see my shop's sales from my phone when I am away?

Yes. Sales, stock and top products can be viewed remotely, and a daily summary can arrive on WhatsApp at closing time. Owners with several branches use this to compare shops without travelling to each one.

Can one system run several shops or branches?

It can, with stock and prices kept per branch and a combined owner view. Branches sync to a central account, so a transfer between shops is recorded rather than guessed, and each branch keeps selling if the link drops.

Is my sales data safe if the laptop is stolen?

Online backups make theft a hardware loss, not a business loss: a new machine is installed and the day's figures restore from the cloud. Keep a nightly backup and record who holds admin rights so a stolen till cannot be opened by a stranger.

How long does POS setup take?

Two days to a week for a single shop: importing your product list, setting prices and discounts, connecting the printer, and training the cashiers. Pharmacies and restaurants with modifiers or expiry batches take a little longer.

Do my staff need training to use it?

A cashier needs about an hour to learn selling, holding a sale and reprinting a receipt. The training that matters is on exceptions: refunds, credit sales, stock corrections and end-of-day closing. We train the supervisor separately for those.

Can it handle credit sales and customers who owe?

Yes. Each sale can be on cash or credit against a customer account, with a running balance and a statement. Shops that sell on credit lose money quietly in exercise books; a ledger shows exactly who owes what and since when.
