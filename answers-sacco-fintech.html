SACCO and microfinance questions, answered

For SACCO managers, treasurers and boards moving from ledgers to software: what it costs, what it must do, and how to avoid the traps.

What is a SACCO?

A Savings and Credit Cooperative Organisation is a member-owned financial group: members buy shares, save regularly, and borrow from the pool at agreed interest. In Uganda they are registered with the Ministry of Trade as cooperatives and supervised through the department of cooperatives.

How do I start a SACCO in Uganda?

Agree the common bond and by-laws, register the group as a cooperative, open a bank account, elect a board and set the savings and loan policy. Registration requires at least 30 members in most districts and a constitution, then annual returns once running.

What is SACCO software?

Software that keeps member records, shares, savings, loan disbursements, interest, repayments and the daily cash book in one system, and prints the reports a SACCO must show at the annual general meeting. It replaces the ledger books and the treasurer's spreadsheet.

How much does SACCO software cost in Uganda?

Small SACCOs pay about UGX 1,500,000 to 4,000,000 for a one-off system, while multi-branch SACCOs pay more or move to a monthly subscription of UGX 150,000 to 500,000. Training and data entry from the old books is usually included in the first month.

What features should SACCO software have?

Member register with photos, share and savings ledgers, loan application and approval workflow, interest and penalty calculation, repayment schedules, cash book, and reports for the AGM. Anything that cannot produce a trial balance is a record book, not accounting software.

Can members check their balance on their phone?

Yes. Members can see savings, loan balance and the next repayment date through USSD or a simple app without calling the office. That single feature removes most of the phone traffic a treasurer handles every market day.

Can the system work offline at a branch?

It should. Rural branches lose network daily, so transactions save locally and sync when the connection returns. Offline-first design is the difference between software a SACCO can run in Kyenjojo and software that only works in Kampala.

How does it handle shares, savings and loans?

Separate ledgers per member, with shares that do not move, savings that earn interest, and loans with a schedule that recalculates on early or late payment. Interest is calculated by the rule in your by-laws, not by hand at month end.

Can it integrate with mobile money?

Yes. MTN and Airtel collection APIs let members save straight from their phone, and the transaction posts to the right member account automatically. That reduces the cash handling and the disputes about whether money reached the office.

How does the software stop staff fraud?

Every transaction records who entered it and when, receipts cannot be deleted quietly, day-end balancing must match cash, and the board sees the audit trail. Most fraud needs a gap between the cash and the record, and this closes it.

Do members need smartphones?

No. Balances can be checked by USSD on any phone, and the office keeps a member register with photos. Smartphone apps help younger members but should never be the only way to reach an account.

How long does it take to move from paper books?

Two to six weeks depending on how many members and how clean the old books are. We load the member register and opening balances first, then run one month in parallel so the board can compare software totals against the manual cash book.

Which reports does a SACCO need?

Member statements, savings and shares summary, loan portfolio and ageing, interest income, cash book and bank reconciliation, plus the income and expenditure statement and balance sheet for the AGM. Regulators and auditors ask for these first.

Can one system run several branches?

Yes, with each branch keeping its own cash book while members see one balance across branches. Head office sees consolidated figures, and transactions from a branch that was offline appear once it syncs.

What is the difference between a SACCO and a microfinance?

A SACCO is owned by its members and lends their pooled savings. A microfinance is a company lending its own or borrowed capital, often with higher interest and no membership shares. Both need the same loan tracking, but only the SACCO has share capital and member voting.
